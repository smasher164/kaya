# The four demo apps' own looks: the rulings and the build

Status: RULED 2026-11-03 (the maintainer, on the "Demo App Looks" page,
https://claude.ai/artifact/2xgfgt6jXt8VyshJXcUYVg). Every ruling below is
theirs; the build order and the settled choices (§4) are the build's. The
styling rules this plan lives under are docs/styling-pass-plan.md's Y1-Y11
(grounds, pictures, shapes, tints and states are the app's skin; the
controls stay the platform's; no app branches on the platform) and
docs/tints-plan.md (T1-T3). The research behind the page's options is in
the 2026-10-30 session's scratchpad (ownlook/research.md); its one
correction to the brief, that Things 3 has no per-area colours, stands.

## §1 The rulings (2026-11-03)

The four apps, one option each, in the maintainer's words where they ruled
in words:

- **Task manager** (guests/rust/tasks.rs): option A, a colour per list. The
  header of each list washes from the list's colour (Y2's gradient), the
  title large and bold, the count under it; dates tinted overdue red, today
  accent, within two days amber; each project led by a swatch of its colour.
  PLUS the Reminders-style grid of tiles (option B's shape) on the Inbox: a
  2x2 grid of cards (Today, Upcoming, Anytime, Logbook), each in its list's
  own colour, a symbol in its corner, a big count over its name, a tap
  opening that list.
- **Chat** (guests/go/chat): option A, a wallpaper per conversation,
  generated by a script beside tools/gen-avatars.py (abstract gradients and
  shapes, seeded from each avatar's colours; no photos, nothing downloaded),
  bound to the thread's ground per conversation; the bubbles keep their
  fills; any bare caption over the picture goes in the overlay fill (Y3).
- **Portfolio** (guests/python/portfolio.py): option A, the day's colour:
  the hero washes green on an up day and red on a down day, the total big,
  the change on a filled green or red pill, the same pill per holding; AND
  the chart's line in the day's colour through the new paints (Q3). Option
  B's watchlist rows stay possible later; not built now.
- **Editor** (guests/go/editor): MERGE A and B. By default A: a bundled
  open-licence coding face (JetBrains Mono or IBM Plex Mono, with its
  licence file) on the buffer on every platform, and the status bar a solid
  accent strip with the pair's white text. A word count always in the
  status bar. A View menu toggle "Writing Mode" that switches the buffer to
  a writing face a size up, hides the line numbers, and centres the text at
  a fixed reading measure. Focus mode is NOT built.

The page's questions:

- **Q2, YES: the data-colour tier** (docs/tints-plan.md T3). A small
  container may be filled with an app's own colour, kaya choosing a readable
  text colour on it, contrast-checked, light and dark.
- **Q3, YES: the canvas gains two paints, up and down**, the platform's own
  green and red (see §4's note on what "the platform's own" can mean inside
  the canvas).
- **Q6: wallpapers generated by script**, as above.
- **NEW, a standalone icon** (the maintainer: "wouldn't we need a card to
  hold an icon for that grid to be buildable?"). Today kaya draws a symbol
  only on a button, a menu item, a swipe item or a tab. An element that
  draws a vocabulary symbol anywhere (in a card, a row, an empty state),
  with a size and a tint, lowered to SwiftUI `Image(systemName:)`, Compose
  `Icon`, `GtkImage` by icon name and WinUI `FontIcon`/`SymbolIcon`, through
  the existing six-file symbol vocabulary (tools/check-symbol-parity.py,
  tools/check-symbols.py).
- **NEW, a reading measure**: a textarea can cap its line length (a maximum
  width with the text centred in a wider box), for Writing Mode.

Questions the page asked that these rulings leave as they were: Q4 (the
editor is both, merged above); Q5 (an always-dark app) is not taken up, the
demos follow the system.

## §3 The slices

### §3.1 The standalone icon (framework piece a)

BUILT 2026-11-04: kind `icon` (28) on all five backends and in all nine bindings with the C floor's generated floor; the `icons` scene (rust) green on every lane, its two SwiftUI negatives watched red (the drawn glyph swapped, the tint dropped). WinUI's code-surface face handler now leaves a non-text kind's `text_scale` to that kind's own arm, which the windows leg found.

A new kind, `icon` (28): a widget that draws one symbol of the closed
vocabulary and nothing else. Its props:

- `symbol` (41), the glyph; required. The root refuses an icon mounted with
  no symbol, at the transaction, the way a canvas with no accessible name is
  refused. Bindable in the template zone to a row's symbol field, as a
  button's is.
- `text_scale` (72), its size: the glyph draws at the platform's body text
  size times the scale, so it composes with the user's text-size setting as
  every text kind does (docs/code-surface-plan.md S1). 1 is unset.
- `tint` (66), its colour, Y7's standalone tints; inside a `filled`
  container the icon takes the pair's foreground and the tint is ignored, as
  a label does. `neutral` and `overlay` refused, as on a label.
- `a11y_label`: with one the icon is an image to assistive technology with
  that name; without one it is decorative and hidden from the tree, since a
  bare glyph beside its own caption (a tile's name) would be read twice.

Legal nowhere else: `text` is refused on it (the title stays the button's).
Lowering: SwiftUI `Image(systemName:)` with `.font(.body)` scaled and
`.foregroundStyle` from the tint or the fill's pair; Compose `Icon` with the
vector the button table already names, sized from the body size; GTK
`gtk::Image::from_icon_name` with `pixel_size` from the body size, and the
tint's style class; WinUI `FontIcon` with the glyph the button table names,
`FontSize` from the body size and the tint's brush. All four read the ONE
table each backend keeps for buttons, so the vocabulary stays six files.

Reads: `expect_symbol icon@<id> "<name>"`, the existing verb, whose target
set grows by `icon`. The reader on each backend answers from the drawn
glyph (the SF name mapped back, the vector's name, the icon name, the glyph
code), never from the node's prop. Watched red with the SwiftUI arm's glyph
replaced.

Scene: an `icons` scene beside the tints scene, in its shape: an icon per
tint, one scaled, one inside an accent fill, one with a name and one
without, read on every lane in every language.

### §3.2 The data-colour fill (framework piece b, T3)

BUILT 2026-11-04: `filled color` on all five backends and in all nine bindings; the tints scene reads two live fills and a stamped one through `expect_fill_color`, light and dark; the core's inverted foreground choice watched red on all three. Measured: the ink scan must stay 10 units inside the frame, since the rounded corners show the page through the frame's rectangle and a dark page read as black ink on an amber fill (harness.rs FILL_INK_INSET).

The tint vocabulary grows by `color` (7). `filled` set to `color` on a row
or column takes its fill from the existing `color` prop (44), the colour
picker's and the gradient ground's, made legal on row and column when
`filled` is `color` as Y2 makes it legal when `ground` is `gradient`;
`color` with neither is refused at the root. The container is otherwise the
filled container of docs/tints-plan.md: the platform's card radius, the
card inset unless the app set one, the foreground inherited by everything
inside.

The foreground is kaya's choice, in the core, from the fill alone: white or
black, whichever has the greater WCAG contrast ratio against the fill
(crates/kaya/src/brand.rs's `contrast_ratio`), the same in both
appearances since the fill is the app's colour in both. The core sends the
pair to the backend in the `color` prop as Y2 sends the gradient's two
stops: the fill in bits 24-47, the foreground in bits 0-23. A label, a
caption, an icon or a link inside takes the foreground as it takes the
accent fill's on-accent colour today (the same route, docs/tints-plan.md
§4.1 for WinUI's per-label style).

Why white-or-black and not a tinted text colour: the page's ruling is
"kaya choosing a readable text colour"; the two extremes are the only
colours that are legible on every fill a contrast check admits, and they
are what Reminders' tiles and Stocks' pills draw.

Reads: `expect_fill_color <container> "RRGGBB on white|black"`: the fill
read at `expect_fill`'s probe (4 units inside the right edge at mid height)
within the ground-colour tolerance, and the text colour read off the first
label inside through the same resolved-colour route `expect_tint` uses.
The verdict names the hex the app chose, so the scene is byte-identical on
every lane. Watched red with the core's foreground choice inverted.

Template zone: `filled` is a prop on the template node as today and
`color` binds to a row's colour field (the ground's precedent), so a
project row's swatch is one filled column bound to the project's colour.

Scene: tools/scenes/tints.steps grows two containers, one light fill (black
text) and one dark fill (white text).

This closes docs/deferred.md's "the data-colour tier beyond the gradient"
entry; the task manager is its first consumer.

### §3.8 The reading measure (framework piece d)

`max_width` (42), the image's and the slider's bound, made legal on the
textarea: the text column is at most this wide, in DIP, centred in the

centred; the editor's Writing Mode reads it at 480 (§4). The SwiftUI measure

first cut hugged the field to its text (120 of 328): the column's box hands

## §4 Choices the build settled

Mechanical choices the rulings left open, each one named here so a later
reader does not take it for a ruling:

- Kind `icon` = 28 (the kind table ends at `surface` 27). Named `icon`
  rather than `symbol` because every binding already spells `symbol` as the
  prop setter on buttons and template nodes (Rust's `Tpl::symbol(node, src)`
  would collide with a constructor of that name), and because the maintainer
  called it an icon.
- Tint `color` = 7 (`overlay` is 6). Paints `up` = 6, `down` = 7,
  `up_fill` = 8, `down_fill` = 9.
- The icon's size is `text_scale`, not a point size: every text kind's
  size is a multiple of the platform's (S1), and a glyph that did not scale
  with the user's text size would be the one thing on the screen that did
  not.
- The data fill's foreground is white or black by contrast ratio, the same
  in both appearances; the fill is not clamped (the gradient's clamp exists
  because the gradient's text is the appearance's primary colour; here the
  text follows the fill).
- The paints' values (§3.3): light `up` 209D55 (1F9D55 at first, moved one
  unit of red because a Mac's Display P3 window returns 1F as 1D, past
  `expect_ink`'s ±1; docs/traps.md), `down` D93A2B, dark `up`
  4FD37A, `down` FF6B5E, fills at the series fill's alpha.
- The task lists' colours: Inbox 2F6FED, Today F2B01E, Upcoming D9486A,
  Anytime 1FA2A0, Logbook 2E9E5B; the project palette, by order: 8E6FE0,
  E07A3F, 1D6F8C, C64B8C, 5B9A2F, 8A6E4B. Y2's clamp decides the wash in
  each appearance; the tiles and swatches draw the colour itself.
- The portfolio's hero colours are the paints' own values for up and down,
  so the wash, the pills and the line agree; flat keeps the mark's blue, and
  a holding that did not move wears grey 8E8E93. kaya's contrast rule puts
  black text on all three pill colours (D93A2B is a near tie at 4.58 against
  4.59).
- The wallpapers are 960x960: cover-cropped, a square loses the least on
  a landscape pane and a portrait phone alike.
- The editor's writing face is the platform's own text face: the ruling
  named a coding face and said "a writing face" without naming one, and a
  second bundled font is weight the slice does not need. The reading
  measure is 480 DIP, about 60 characters of the writing face a size up:
  narrow enough to show inside the editor's 640-wide window, where a 640
  measure would never take effect and the scene's read could not tell it
  from none. On a phone the box is narrower than the measure, and the text
  takes the whole box.
- The reading measure rides `max_width`, in DIP, rather than a new prop in
  characters: the prop exists, its meaning (a cap, never a stretch) is the
  same, and a measure in characters would move with every face and scale.
- The icon without `a11y_label` is decorative and hidden from assistive
  technology; with one, an image with that name.

### Q-L1: a wallpaper in the dark appearance (RULED 2026-11-04: C)

The chat review captures show it. The wallpapers are pale (the avatar's
colours moved most of the way toward white), and in the dark appearance the
thread keeps that pale picture while the page around it goes dark. On
macOS, iOS, GTK and WinUI the neutral bubble is a translucent fill with the
appearance's light text, so the other person's messages are pale text on a
pale picture and cannot be read. Compose's neutral fill is opaque, so its
bubbles stay legible, but the thread is still a bright panel in a dark
window. The rulings said "a wallpaper per conversation" and did not say what
it does in the dark.

- A. Each wallpaper gets a dark twin (the same gradient and motif moved most
  of the way toward the dark page instead of white), and an image ground
  takes an optional second picture for the dark appearance, lowered to each
  platform's own appearance variants (an asset catalogue's dark appearance,
  Android's `-night` resources, a WinUI theme dictionary, GTK's dark style)
  and switched live when the appearance changes. New prop, one picture per
  appearance, the app supplies both.
- B. kaya dims every image ground in the dark appearance with a scrim in the
  page's colour at a fixed opacity, as WhatsApp's wallpaper dimming does.
  One picture; also darkens the music app's artist photo in the dark.
- C. A fill over a picture is always opaque (the fill composed over the
  page colour), so the bubbles read in both appearances; the wallpaper stays
  bright in a dark window.

RULED C (the maintainer, 2026-11-04: "yeah let's do C"). A rejected: "if the
user set a wallpaper for example, then A wouldn't be viable", since a picture
the user chose has no dark twin. B rejected: "it wouldn't just affect the chat
wallpaper but also other apps that have stuff like album art. im not sure how
i feel about that". The bubbles were see-through because the platforms'
neutral fills are translucent on macOS, iOS, GTK and WinUI, which is invisible
over a plain page; Compose's is opaque. A later per-ground opt-in dim is a
note, not work.

BUILT 2026-11-04: a fill whose nearest ground is a picture is composed over the
page colour of the current appearance: SwiftUI draws the window ground under
the fill's shape, GTK's sheet gives a filled container under a
`kaya-picture-ground` the page as its background colour and the fill as an

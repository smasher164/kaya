measured 2026-08-27, while landing the canvas chart (phase 4)
the obligation: docs/canvas-plan.md §11 phase 4, "the palette of §6 is

THE PROBLEM THIS SOLVES
-----------------------
A capture shows ONE mode: the host's. This mac is light, and switching
the machine's appearance to review the other half is not something a
session should do to someone's desktop. `expect_ink` freezes the dark
palette's BYTES, so the numbers are asserted — but nobody had LOOKED at
the dark chart, and §7.3's whole point is that every scene assertion was
green through the two look bugs the portfolio has already shipped.

HOW IT WAS DONE, WITHOUT TOUCHING THE HOST
------------------------------------------
The core renders the display raster per mode already, so the review is a
render rather than a screenshot:

1. The guest's REAL op stream is captured through the binding — the one
   `wire.tx_set_drawing` call is wrapped, its arguments recorded, and the
   guest aborted before it opens a window. 220 ops, viewbox 280x180,
   which is the count tools/scenes/portfolio.steps freezes, so the stream
   under review is the one the mac leg asserts.
2. A scratch #[test] spliced into crates/kaya/src/canvas.rs (run, file
   restored by hash) validates that stream and rasterizes it at scale
   2.0 for Mode::Light and Mode::Dark.
3. The gutters carry no ground fill — the ground rect is the PLOT rect —
   so each raster is composited over the window ground it will sit on
   before being written as a PNG. Light is EEF1F2, MEASURED off the mac
   capture. DARK IS 1E1E1E, STATED (macOS's dark window background) and
   NOT measured on this host. That assumption is the one thing below
   that a dark machine could still overturn.

WHAT WAS TRIED AND DID NOT WORK: `-AppleInterfaceStyle Dark` in the
guest's own arguments, which is the per-process appearance override and
would have given a real dark window. The captured window came back
light (EEF1F2 at the same pixels as the ordinary run), so the override
did not reach this stack. Recorded so the next person does not spend the
same ten minutes.

RESOLVED 2026-08-28, and the argument-domain route was the wrong lever
rather than a broken one. `-AppleInterfaceStyle Dark` writes the
process's NSUserDefaults argument domain; what AppKit resolves an
appearance from is `NSApp.appearance`, which that domain does not feed.
Setting it directly does work, and is now the knob: `KAYA_APPEARANCE=dark`
makes ONE PROCESS adopt an appearance through each platform's own
supported override — `NSApp.appearance` on macOS,
`overrideUserInterfaceStyle` on iOS, libadwaita's forced colour scheme on
GTK, `FrameworkElement.RequestedTheme` on WinUI (element scope: the
application-scope property throws once the app is running) and
and on Android the window background resolved out of the `-night` theme
together with `LocalConfiguration`'s night bits. No host setting is
written, so the "not something a session should do to someone's desktop"
constraint above is met rather than worked around.

(Android's app-level `UiModeManager.setApplicationNightMode` was the
first mechanism and is NOT the one that shipped: it relaunches the
activity, `onCreate` runs twice in one process, and the second mount dies
on a duplicate widget id. Measured on the lane 2026-08-27; the
re-entrancy gap it exposed is its own ledger entry.)

WHAT THAT CHANGES FOR THIS DOCUMENT: the dark render below is still a
composite over a STATED window ground (1E1E1E), and that assumption is
still the one thing a dark machine could overturn — but it no longer
needs a dark machine. `KAYA_APPEARANCE=dark` on the mac canvas leg
produces a real dark window on this light desk, so §3's open question is
answerable by a capture whenever the maintainer wants to look at it. The
knob is held by tools/check-appearance.py and exercised by the
`canvasdark-*` leg on all five lanes.

WHAT I SEE, AND THE ONE THING THAT IS NOT SETTLED
-------------------------------------------------
1. NO PALETTE VALUE WAS NUDGED, and the measurement is why. Dark is
   STRONGER than light on six of the seven rows — the series stands off
   its ground at 7.21 against light's 4.77 — and the one row where it is
   weaker rests on the assumed window ground. Ruling 13 makes these
   values tweakable pixels; there was nothing here to justify tweaking.
2. MY EYE WAS WRONG ABOUT THE GRIDLINES and the pixels corrected it.
   Looking at the dark render I read the gridlines as vanishing into the
   area fill. They do not: dark's gridline-on-fill is 1.34 against
   light's 1.24. What changes is POLARITY — light's gridline goes DARKER
   than the fill it crosses, dark's goes LIGHTER — and that reads as
   disappearance without being it. Written down because the obvious
   "fix" would have made the dark gridlines louder than the light ones
   for no measured reason.
3. THE ONE OPEN QUESTION IS THE PANEL'S RELATIONSHIP TO ITS WINDOW.
   In light the plot ground is WHITE on a EEF1F2 window: a raised card,
   matching the tables beside it. In dark the plot ground 16181C is
   DARKER than any plausible macOS dark window: a recess, the opposite
   reading, at the weakest separation in the table (1.07). Whether that
   is right is a taste question AND a consistency question, because the
   tables next to the chart draw their card in the PLATFORM's own token
   (tools/check-table-card.py) while the canvas draws in kaya's palette
   — so on a dark host the chart could sit visibly darker than the
   cards it is beside. NOT DECIDABLE HERE: it needs a dark machine, one
   capture, and the maintainer's eye on whether a chart well should read
   raised or recessed.

HOW TO REDO IT
--------------
The three scratch scripts are not checked in (a capture harness that
splices a test into the crate is not something to leave lying in
tools/). Rebuild from the description above: wrap
`kaya.wire.tx_set_drawing`, run the guest under runpy, abort from the
wrapper; write the values out tagged one per line; read them back in a
spliced #[test], `validate` then `rasterize` at scale 2.0 per mode,
composite over the window ground, `Pixmap::save_png`.

THE GPU PATH AGAINST THREADED VELLO_CPU, AT KAYA'S SIZES
measured 2026-09-10, opening docs/canvas-gpu-plan.md's slice 2 (ruling G3)
the obligation: "if the GPU is not decisively ahead of threaded vello_cpu
at those sizes, slice 2 stops here and the record says so"

THE RESULT: THE GPU LOSES AT EVERY SIZE, AND SLICE 2 STOPS
-----------------------------------------------------------

(load under 8 at the run), one RenderContext / Scene reused across
frames so no thread pool or pipeline is paid per frame:

Read across a row: `cpu MT` (vello_cpu on 8 worker threads, the context
reused) is the fastest column at every size from 1.6 megapixels up, and
`cpu 1T` is the fastest below that; `gpu render` (vello_hybrid 0.2.0 on
wgpu 29 / Metal, no readback, the frame joined with a device poll) is
3-7x slower than cpu MT and slower than cpu 1T at every size but one
(the 2000 octagons on the desk, where the GPU render alone is 3.08ms
against 2.16ms single-threaded and 0.85ms threaded); `gpu+readback`
adds the copy_texture_to_buffer + map_async + de-pad and is the number a
canvas that hands bytes to a blit arm would pay. The `build` column is
the CPU half of the hybrid frame (path processing into strips) and by
itself is already most of a cpu MT frame.

So at 280x180 the chart is 0.08ms on one thread against 1.63ms through
the GPU with readback: twenty times slower. At the phone's 1200x2400 the
worst case measured, 2000 antialiased octagons, is 0.88ms threaded
against 3.67ms through the GPU. Nothing here is within a factor of two
of "decisively ahead", so ruling G3's named stop applies: SLICE 2 ENDS
WITH "vello_cpu with threads, no GPU". No device policy, no probe scene,
no second renderer to keep in step, and the linux lane's software GL
question never arises.

WHAT THE THREADS ARE WORTH, WHICH IS THE PART THAT SURVIVES
-----------------------------------------------------------
The determinism record measured threads SLOWER than one at small sizes
with a fresh context per raster (the pool was rebuilt each time). With
the context reused, the crossover is at about a megapixel: below it one
thread wins (0.08 vs 0.23ms on the native chart; 0.70 vs 0.64ms on the
native text page is the edge), above it 8 threads are 1.7-2.5x faster
(0.42 -> 0.25, 1.69 -> 0.84, 2.16 -> 0.85, 1.95 -> 0.88ms). Every
phone-size frame is under a millisecond threaded, against a 16.7ms
budget. And the threaded bytes EQUALLED the single-threaded bytes in
every cell (the probe compares them and would have printed
`[MT bytes != 1T bytes]`), which the determinism record had already
found for 2 and 4 threads; 8 is now measured too.

What that makes the screen raster's remaining work: a RenderContext kept
per thread and resized rather than rebuilt, threads switched on above a
measured pixel count, `multithreading` enabled on the vello_cpu
dependency. The canonical raster stays scalar and single-threaded
(G2). That is a small slice of its own; nothing in this record needs a
ruling.

THE TOLERANCE NUMBERS, FOR THE RECORD
-------------------------------------
Had the GPU path been taken, `expect_drawing_matches` would have needed:
the hybrid's bytes differ from vello_cpu's by at most 1 per channel on
the chart and the text at native and desk sizes, at most 2 on the
chart's large sizes, and at most 5 on the octagons, on 0.4-16.7% of the
pixels, all of it at antialiased edges. vello's own `hybrid_tolerance`
of 1-3 and the probe scene's tolerance of 3 sit in the same place.

WHAT WAS RUN
------------
canvas-gpu-timing-2026-09-10.rs beside this file, the determinism
probe's op streams and text pipeline plus a 2000-octagon stress scene
(docs/canvas-plan.md §15.2's headroom unit), each fitted by kaya's own
uniform-fit rule into the three tracks: the drawing's own viewbox, the
linux lane's 1600x1000, and a phone's 1200x2400. Renderers: vello_cpu
0.2.0 (u8 pipeline, Level::new() = NEON, num_threads 0 and 8, context
reset and reused), and vello_hybrid 0.2.0 on wgpu 29.0.3 through Metal
on the Apple M5 Pro (Features::empty(), Rgba8Unorm render target,
RENDER_ATTACHMENT | COPY_SRC, readback into a MAP_READ buffer with
256-byte row padding removed). `cargo run --release` in the dev shell.

THE CAVEATS, SO NOBODY OVER-READS THIS
--------------------------------------
One host, one GPU, and it is unified memory: the readback is a same-
memory copy here, so a discrete GPU would make the `gpu+readback` column
WORSE, never better. The GPU's fixed per-frame cost (command encoding,
submit, the poll) dominates at these sizes; a scene of hundreds of
thousands of paths could cross over, and the octagon row's trend (the
GPU's disadvantage shrinking from 20x to 4x as the CPU work grows) says
where — but that is not a canvas kaya draws, and the moment it is, this
probe is a `cargo run` away. The `gpu render` column joins the frame
with a blocking poll; a presented surface would pipeline instead, which
helps throughput and not the latency a chart re-raster cares about.

WHERE A HYBRID FRAME'S TIME GOES, AND WHAT PIPELINING BUYS
----------------------------------------------------------
Taken after the maintainer asked why the GPU was so much slower and
whether an initial overhead would amortize. One frame split into its
four parts (median of 15 after 3 warm-ups), then a burst of 20 frames
encoded and submitted back to back with ONE wait at the end, per frame:

    drawing                    track               strips    encode    submit      wait       frame |    burst/frame   cpu MT ref
    portfolio chart (214 ops)  native              0.08ms    0.09ms    0.04ms    1.52ms      1.74ms |         0.33ms       0.17ms
    portfolio chart (214 ops)  phone 1200x2400     0.17ms    0.11ms    0.05ms    1.52ms      1.86ms |         0.59ms       0.28ms
    text page (34 ops)         native              0.72ms    0.09ms    0.04ms    1.52ms      2.39ms |         0.89ms       0.62ms
    text page (34 ops)         phone 1200x2400     1.27ms    0.17ms    0.05ms    1.52ms      3.01ms |         1.48ms       0.76ms
    2000 octagons (20006 ops)  native              0.82ms    0.24ms    0.04ms    1.52ms      2.66ms |         1.19ms       0.76ms
    2000 octagons (20006 ops)  phone 1200x2400     1.04ms    0.43ms    0.08ms    1.54ms      3.18ms |         1.61ms       0.92ms

Read it this way. `strips` is the CPU half of the hybrid — flattening
the curves and generating the sparse strips with their coverage — and
it is the same front end vello_cpu runs, so it is never smaller than a
good part of a whole vello_cpu frame. `encode` and `submit` are small.
`wait` is 1.52ms IN EVERY ROW, independent of the drawing and of the
pixel count: it is the round trip for a Metal command buffer to be
scheduled, run and signal completion, a latency and not work. The
one-time cost (pipeline compilation) is outside these numbers, paid in
the warm-ups. So the per-frame cost does NOT amortize with more frames
of the same kind — every joined frame pays the 1.5ms — but it does hide
under PIPELINING: with 20 frames in flight and one wait the burst
figure is 0.33-1.61ms per frame, which is the CPU half plus the GPU's
share of the throughput. That is still above 8-thread vello_cpu in
every row (0.17-0.92ms), because at these sizes the fine rasterization
the GPU takes over is cheap on eight cores, and the hybrid's CPU half
remains. What would move the balance is MORE WORK PER FRAME of the kind
the GPU scales on — large overlapping fills, gradients, images, blend
layers, tens of thousands of paths — not more frames; kaya's canvases
are hundreds of ops, and a presented surface would hide the latency
without changing which side finishes first.

THE FILL-HEAVY CASE, WHICH IS THE CASE THE GPU IS FOR
-----------------------------------------------------
Taken on the maintainer's next question: is this a chicken-and-egg,
where the heavy canvases never get written because there is no GPU
path, and would the plan's slice 3 (a presented surface, no readback)
have changed the verdict? Scenes of N large overlapping quads, each
covering ~60% of the box and half of them the translucent series fill
so every pixel blends many times — the shape a GPU fine stage scales on
and a CPU fine stage does not — at kaya's own paint vocabulary (solid
colours; no gradients, images or blurs exist in it). `gpu burst/fr` is
the presented-surface shape: 20 frames in flight, one wait, no readback.

    drawing          track               cpu 1T    cpu MT   gpu frame  gpu burst/fr strips (cpu)
    10 big fills     phone 1200x2400     0.61ms    0.41ms      1.65ms        0.42ms       0.14ms
    10 big fills     desk 1600x1000      0.52ms    0.35ms      1.63ms        0.43ms       0.12ms
    50 big fills     phone 1200x2400     1.51ms    0.61ms      2.35ms        1.04ms       0.60ms
    50 big fills     desk 1600x1000      1.30ms    0.53ms      2.31ms        1.00ms       0.53ms
    200 big fills    phone 1200x2400     4.96ms    1.45ms      6.14ms        3.87ms       2.48ms
    200 big fills    desk 1600x1000      4.66ms    1.45ms      6.26ms        4.21ms       2.39ms
    800 big fills    phone 1200x2400    18.77ms    5.10ms     18.49ms       15.28ms      10.16ms
    800 big fills    desk 1600x1000     17.51ms    4.73ms     18.83ms       13.73ms       9.30ms

Two readings. First, vello_cpu on 8 threads draws 800 full-screen
translucent fills at phone resolution in 5.1ms, a third of the 16.7ms
frame budget, and that is far beyond anything a UI canvas draws, so the
egg is large before any GPU is needed. Second, and the reason the
verdict does not flip even in the presented shape: THE HYBRID'S CPU
HALF SCALES WITH THE SCENE TOO — its strip generation alone is 10.2ms
at 800 fills, twice vello_cpu's whole threaded frame — so the hybrid is
CPU-bound exactly where a GPU was supposed to help, and pipelining
cannot hide a cost that is on the CPU. At every N the burst figure is
above cpu MT (0.42 vs 0.41 at 10 fills, 15.3 vs 5.1 at 800). The
renderer that escapes this is the ALL-GPU one, classic `vello`
(compute shaders for flattening and binning as well as fine), which
the vello project keeps under research/ and calls experimental, needs
compute (no GL ES, no WebGL2), and is what to measure if a canvas ever
outgrows vello_cpu on threads; the presentation mechanisms in
docs/canvas-gpu-plan.md §5 apply to it unchanged, since either
renderer ends in a wgpu texture.

The caveat that stands from §15.3: these are M5 Pro cores. A phone's
efficiency cores are slower and fewer, so the CPU's frame at 800 fills
is more like 12-15ms there by the plan's own scaling caution — inside
budget, and still ahead of a hybrid whose CPU half doubles with it.
Measure on a phone-class core before believing the ratio.

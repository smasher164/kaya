THE AUTOMERGE BENCHMARK: docs/rich-text-plan.md's PROTOCOL DRIVEN AGAINST automerge 0.11.0
measured 2026-09-11, on the maintainer's question ("how well this would
integrate with something like automerge. That's probably the benchmark.")
tools/richtext/automerge-probe, `cargo run --release`

WHAT WAS BUILT
--------------
A model of kaya's rich mirror — the text plus per-byte attributes, with
the widget's inheritance rule stated once (typed text inherits the
inline attributes of the byte before it, except a link, which never
grows by typing) — driven by the plan's messages: `text_edited` and
`text_formatted` up, `apply_edit` and format down. Beside it an
automerge document built with TextEncoding::Utf8CodeUnit, kaya's ruled
unit, and a bridge an app would write: a local edit becomes
`splice_text(pos, del, text)` with the patch log on, the splice's own
patch says what automerge's expand rules put on the inserted text, and
the bridge marks or unmarks whatever differs from the widget's runs
(expand policy the app's: After for inline styles, None for links).
Automerge's patches after a merge — SpliceText, DeleteSeq, Mark — become
apply_edit and format messages applied to the mirror, and the local
caret is transformed by R5's rule while automerge's own Cursor is the
oracle. After every step the mirror's text and runs are compared with
automerge's spans().

READ ACROSS
-----------
- OFFSETS ARE IDENTITY. Every position kaya's mirror holds went into
  splice_text and mark unchanged, multi-byte text included (é, an
  emoji, CJK, a combining mark), and every position automerge's
  patches carried came back unchanged. Nothing converts, as the survey
  said (docs/probes/richtext-crdt-2026-09-11.md).
- THE ADDRESSED EDIT IS THE RIGHT SHAPE. Three of automerge's patch
  actions map one to one onto the plan's messages: SpliceText is an
  apply_edit with the inserted text's marks as its runs, DeleteSeq an
  apply_edit with nothing inserted, Mark a format. No delta walk, no
  synthesis.
- TWO PEERS CONVERGE THROUGH KAYA'S MESSAGES. A bolded "quick" and
  typed " jumps"; B inserted "very " before "quick" and italicized
  "fox"; after each merged the other, both mirrors read the same text
  and runs, and both equalled automerge's spans.
- THE CARET RULE IS AUTOMERGE'S. R5's transform (unchanged before the
  edit, shifted after it, a caret at the edit's start ends after the
  insertion) matched automerge's Cursor with MoveCursor::After, its
  documented default, in both the concurrent-merge case and the
  insertion landing exactly at the caret.
- THE INHERITANCE RECONCILE COSTS NOTHING when read from the splice's
  own patch: 0.024ms per bridged edit on a 52 KB document with forty
  bold runs against 0.023ms for a bare splice. Read through a spans()
  walk instead, as the first draft did, it was 0.633ms — the whole
  document's runs per keystroke. A binding's bridge reads the patch.
- ONE THING IS THE APP'S, AND THE PROBE SHOWS WHY KAYA CARRIES NO EXPAND
  FLAG: B's concurrent insertion of "very " at the START of A's bold
  range came out bold under ExpandMark::After (the marked characters'
  anchors and the inserted text resolve on the CRDT's side, not the
  widget's). An app that wants Peritext's exact boundary behaviour
  picks the expand per mark; kaya's mirror follows the document.
- AUTOMERGE ACCEPTS A MARK THAT SPLITS A CODE POINT: a bold on bytes 1..2
  of "héllo" was taken without error. kaya's one-chokepoint validator
  (docs/ranges-units.md §7) refuses that range before it is lowered,
  so an app behind kaya never sends one; the wall is kaya's.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY
----------------------
It is a model of the mirror, not the core: the widget arms (typing
attributes on NSTextView and the others) are what will make the
inheritance rule true on a platform, and §3 of the plan measures them.
loro and yrs are unmeasured here; the survey says their defaults are the
same unit, and a 60-line probe on loro's emitted delta unit is still
owed before anything is said about loro in an example.
